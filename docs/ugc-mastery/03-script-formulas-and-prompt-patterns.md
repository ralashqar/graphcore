# Script Formulas And Prompt Patterns

This document turns the supplied article formulas into reusable GraphCore scripting structures.

## Core Principle

High-performing UGC is usually not built from blank-page creativity. It is built from repeatable script formulas that:

- match the audience's pain and identity
- open a loop fast
- land a clear proof or payoff

## The Three Most Reusable Formulas

### 1. Reframe

Structure:

1. name the behavior the audience already does
2. refuse to shame them for it
3. redirect the behavior toward a better outcome

Why it works:

- identity protection
- emotional recognition
- shareable "I feel seen" energy

Best fits:

- creator-style UGC
- self-improvement
- wellness
- productivity
- mindset content

GraphCore mapping:

- preset family
  - `ugc_creator`
- likely subtype
  - `creator_reframe`
- likely formula family
  - `reframe`
- common triggers
  - `parasocial_reassurance`
  - `belief_reset`

### 2. You're Doing It Wrong

Structure:

1. name a basic daily behavior
2. say the viewer is doing it wrong
3. explain the hidden consequence
4. show the correction

Why it works:

- immediate curiosity gap
- belief reset
- danger without needing extreme visuals

Best fits:

- explainers
- mechanism ads
- faceless visual demos
- productivity, fitness, finance, health

GraphCore mapping:

- preset family
  - `ugc_direct_response_ad` or `ugc_faceless_format`
- likely subtype
  - `ad_mechanism_proof` or `faceless_explainer`
- likely formula family
  - `doing_it_wrong`
  - `mistake_warning`
- common triggers
  - `curiosity_gap`
  - `belief_reset`

### 3. Validation

Structure:

1. say something the viewer already feels
2. make it cleaner, sharper, and more emotionally legible
3. give permission rather than instruction

Why it works:

- high save/share potential
- low friction
- strong parasocial bond

Best fits:

- creator identity content
- relationship and emotional content
- lifestyle and healing formats

GraphCore mapping:

- preset family
  - `ugc_creator`
- likely subtype
  - `creator_validation`
- likely formula family
  - `validation`
- common triggers
  - `parasocial_reassurance`
  - `social_proof`

## Additional High-Value Formula Families

GraphCore already supports several adjacent formulas that the supplied articles reinforce:

- `problem_solution`
  - the classic pain to relief arc
- `mechanism_proof`
  - show the specific working reason, then prove it
- `before_after`
  - transformation through visible state change
- `contrast_comparison`
  - two options, one winner
- `contrast_narrative`
  - escalating multi-scene comparison engine
- `personal_confession`
  - vulnerable opener that earns trust

## Recommended Shot Job Sequence

Most strong UGC clips still need the same core jobs handled in some form:

1. hook
2. setup or pain
3. mechanism or belief shift
4. proof
5. payoff
6. CTA

Not every clip needs six separate shots, but every successful clip usually covers those jobs somehow.

## Writing Rules For GraphCore Scripts

### Use literal visual language

Shots should describe what is on screen, not polished ad prose.

Prefer:

- "she opens the app and the full week is already mapped"
- "two receipts sit side by side, one much higher than the other"

Avoid:

- "she finally feels in control"
- "the product becomes her secret weapon"

### Give each shot one job

A weak UGC shot often tries to do too much:

- pain
- proof
- CTA
- emotion
- product explanation

A stronger shot has one main assignment.

### Put the product to work

If a product appears, it should usually do something:

- open
- track
- compare
- organize
- display proof
- trigger the better outcome

Passive product beauty shots are rarely enough for direct-response UGC.

### Make proof visible

Good proof cues:

- receipts
- before/after states
- side-by-side comparison
- app screens
- labels
- timestamps
- transformed appearance
- social proof message

Weak proof cues:

- vague happiness
- abstract confidence
- generic smiles without evidence

## Prompt Skeletons

### Reframe skeleton

Use when the viewer already does the behavior and feels judged for it.

Prompt pattern:

- identify the audience behavior
- protect the viewer's identity
- redirect the behavior
- keep the tone like believable creator advice
- end with a soft payoff or soft CTA

### Doing-it-wrong skeleton

Use when a basic habit can be reframed as a hidden mistake.

Prompt pattern:

- open by naming the habit and saying it is being done wrong
- make the downside feel concrete
- explain one overlooked reason
- show the corrected behavior in-frame
- close with clear proof or a short CTA

### Validation skeleton

Use when the main value is emotional recognition.

Prompt pattern:

- say the thought the viewer already has
- sharpen it into a shareable line
- keep the delivery intimate, simple, and direct
- do not over-explain
- close on reassurance, not hard selling

## Format Selection Guidance

Choose formulas based on the goal:

- trust and affinity
  - reframe, validation, personal confession
- education plus conversion
  - doing-it-wrong, mechanism-proof, problem-solution
- pure visual retention
  - before-after, contrast-comparison, contrast-narrative

## Prompt Guidance Rules

When authoring formulas:

1. Start from audience pain or identity tension, not product features.
2. Choose one formula family before writing beats.
3. Keep the first line and first frame aligned around the same hook.
4. Put visible proof before the ending whenever possible.
5. Match the CTA style to the formula. Validation wants low pressure. Direct response can be firmer.

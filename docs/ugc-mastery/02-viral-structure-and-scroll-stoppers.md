# Viral Structure And Scroll-Stoppers

This document distills the supplied articles about virality, first-frame attention, and short-form retention.

## Core Principle

The feed is not evaluating content like a film festival. It is scanning for signals that interrupt scrolling and sustain curiosity.

The practical question is:

- what stops the thumb
- what keeps the loop open
- what produces comments, rewatches, shares, or saves

## Three Main Scroll-Stopping Pillars

### 1. Attraction

Attractive humans stop the scroll quickly because faces and beauty get prioritized fast.

Practical use:

- use characters the audience wants to identify with or reach
- keep them aspirational but not alien
- match the demographic logic of the target audience

Warning:

- unrealistic perfection can create admiration without identification

### 2. Danger

Threat, deterioration, conflict, and risk create vigilance.

Useful danger formats:

- hidden health risk
- hidden productivity loss
- status threat
- missed opportunity
- relationship instability

This does not require horror. It requires meaningful stakes.

### 3. Aesthetics

Pure visual pleasure also holds attention.

Useful forms:

- beautiful lighting
- rich color contrast
- tactile realism
- satisfying motion
- premium environments

Beauty does not replace pain or proof, but it increases the chance that the viewer stays inside the clip long enough to receive them.

## Bonus Pillar: Drama And Social Information

Social conflict is a major retention engine, especially when the viewer wants to know:

- who was right
- who betrayed whom
- who wins
- what the social consequence is

This is the logic behind gossip, relationship dynamics, and serialized interpersonal formats.

## Pattern Interruption

The strongest feed content usually contains a mismatch the brain has to resolve.

Examples:

- childish visual style with adult conflict
- calm visual with alarming narration
- beautiful person naming a sharp pain
- normal daily habit framed as dangerous
- split-screen comparison that feels morally or status-wise loaded

The mismatch is often the actual hook.

## Open Loops

A viewer stays when something unresolved needs closure.

Strong open-loop structures:

- "you are doing this wrong"
- "I did not realize what was happening until..."
- "this one variable changes everything"
- serialized conflict
- before/after with delayed reveal

The hook opens the loop. The middle must keep it open.

## Negativity Bias

Threats and problems often outperform neutral or purely positive framing because the brain gives more weight to downside risk.

Useful translation:

- show the mess before the fix
- show the consequence before the benefit
- show stress before control

Bad translation:

- endless negativity with no payoff
- fake doom with no visible resolution

## Pillar Stacking

The best content usually stacks at least two mechanisms.

Examples:

- attractive creator plus sharp pain
- danger hook plus aesthetic environment
- contrast narrative plus status comparison
- beautiful product demo plus open-loop wrong-belief hook

This is where outsized performance often comes from.

## First-Frame Audit

Every GraphCore UGC clip should be able to answer:

1. What pillar does frame one deploy?
2. What curiosity or tension stays open through the middle?
3. What visible proof or payoff lands before the clip ends?

If the answer to the first question is "none," the clip is weak before script quality even matters.

## Contrast Narrative As A Special Case

One article strongly reinforced a separate category: contrast narrative.

This is the engine behind:

- rich vs poor
- before vs after
- winner vs loser
- skill vs pay-to-win
- glow-up vs stagnation

This format works because it compresses:

- comparison
- status
- escalating tension
- moral clarity
- loopable structure

It is not just normal creator UGC. It is a visual comparison machine.

## GraphCore Mapping

These ideas align directly with existing fields:

- `hookRole`
  - first shot should usually be `hook`
- `dominantTrigger`
  - curiosity gap, status comparison, belief reset, transformation desire
- `targetEmotion`
  - tension, desire, relief, envy, reassurance, urgency
- `contrastAxis`
  - required for contrast-led structures
- `proofMoment`
  - when the loop closes

## Prompt Guidance Rules

When generating short-form scripts:

1. Make frame one stop the scroll through attraction, danger, aesthetics, drama, or a strong contrast combination.
2. Keep one unresolved question alive through the middle.
3. Make the proof visually legible on mute.
4. Do not let the ending become a generic nice-looking shot.
5. For contrast narratives, widen the gap across multiple visible dimensions, not one repeated comparison.

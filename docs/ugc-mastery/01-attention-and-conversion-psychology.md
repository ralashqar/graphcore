# Attention And Conversion Psychology

This document distills the supplied articles about ad psychology, identity, and persuasion into rules that GraphCore can use safely and intentionally.

## Core Principle

Most weak UGC fails before the offer. The viewer has not yet agreed that:

- this is about them
- this person is experiencing something relevant
- this solution has earned the right to appear

The winning order is usually:

1. self-relevance
2. story or situation
3. solution

If the product appears before the viewer feels seen, the pitch usually feels premature.

## The Psychological Contract

Every feed video has a contract:

- the viewer gives attention
- the creative must justify keeping it

The fastest way to break that contract is to sell too early or blame the viewer.

## Four Conversion Mechanisms

### 1. Identity protection

The viewer should not feel accused, stupid, lazy, or at fault.

Better framing:

- "you tried the normal fixes and they did not solve it"
- "the system you were given was incomplete"
- "this is a common problem, not a personal failure"

Why it matters:

- it lowers defensiveness
- it preserves ego
- it keeps the viewer open to a new mechanism

### 2. Knowledge gap

Strong creatives introduce a missing idea, mechanism, or frame the viewer wants resolved.

Examples:

- a named mechanism
- a hidden mistake
- an overlooked cause
- a surprising variable

This keeps attention through the middle of the clip, not just the first second.

### 3. Vicarious skepticism

Objections work best when they are answered before the viewer fully forms them.

Strong patterns:

- another speaker voices the doubt
- the script acknowledges the concern directly
- the answer feels like resolution, not correction

This is especially useful in creator-style conversations, interviews, podcasts, and two-person UGC.

### 4. Loss aversion

The strongest closes often explain what continued inaction costs.

Good loss framing:

- the problem compounds
- the gap widens
- fixing it later gets harder

Weak loss framing:

- melodrama without evidence
- shame-heavy guilt
- fake urgency detached from the product's real use case

## Internal Narrative Identity

People buy in alignment with the story they believe about themselves.

They do not just want:

- a supplement
- a product
- an app

They want help becoming the version of themselves they already imagine:

- disciplined
- caring
- sharp
- attractive
- prepared
- calm under pressure
- socially desired

This means GraphCore should not only identify audience pain. It should identify the heroic self-image the viewer wants validated.

## Do Not Make The Audience The Problem

Bad framing says:

- you are failing
- your habits are bad
- you should feel guilty

Better framing says:

- you were given bad information
- your context makes this hard
- you deserve a better system
- the real villain is the hidden mechanism, bad advice, or unfair environment

This is especially important for wellness, parenting, beauty, productivity, and self-improvement content.

## Sensory Cue Consistency

Identity is not communicated only through words. It also depends on:

- voice
- wardrobe
- camera distance
- environment
- prop choice
- music
- motion
- texture and lighting

If the cues do not match the intended identity, the illusion breaks.

For GraphCore, this means persona style cannot stay abstract. It should influence:

- creator identity refs
- product-hold composites
- environment choice
- framing and camera movement
- style direction in still and video prompts

## Ethical Boundary

The supplied material includes aggressive persuasion language. GraphCore should keep the useful structure while avoiding dishonest or harmful tactics.

Safe line:

- expose a real problem
- frame it clearly
- use selective emphasis without false claims
- make proof visible
- keep the transformation anchored in reality

Unsafe line:

- lie about causality
- invent hidden enemies or fake authority
- weaponize shame against vulnerable users
- imply outcomes the product cannot support

## GraphCore Mapping

These concepts already map well to current GraphCore fields:

- `formulaFamily`
  - reframe, validation, mechanism-proof, mistake-warning
- `dominantTrigger`
  - curiosity gap, belief reset, social proof, parasocial reassurance, guilt pressure, defiance trigger
- `targetEmotion`
  - the response the shot should produce in the viewer
- `personaStyle`
  - the version of the hero speaking on screen
- `proofType`
  - the visible evidence that earns the claim
- `ctaStyle`
  - how the final ask is framed without breaking trust

## Prompt Guidance Rules

When generating UGC:

1. Name the viewer's pain without making them feel defective.
2. Introduce one clear missing mechanism or reframe.
3. Resolve the most predictable objection before the hard sell.
4. Make the cost of inaction legible, but not manipulative.
5. Keep the viewer as the hero and the product as the enabler.
